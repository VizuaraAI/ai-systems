The idea

Start with the problem.
Build the understanding.

A good technical session makes the reasoning visible. What problem were you trying to solve? Which ideas did you build on? What did you try, and what changed your mind?

Take one part of your work and explain it from first principles. Derive the idea, open the code, walk through an experiment. Give people enough depth to reason about it themselves.

You’ll be teaching engineers and researchers enrolled in Vizuara’s technical workshops: an audience ready to follow the details, ask questions, and learn. We teach AI from first principles, and this series brings the people doing the work into that learning process.

Some starting points

What could you teach?

One well-explained idea is enough.

Inference engineering

Where does latency come from? Walk through batching, KV caches, quantization, or a serving system you’ve built.

GPU kernels

Follow a kernel from a first implementation to something fast. Explain the memory access, profiling, and tradeoffs.

Chip design

Connect a learning workload to the hardware that runs it. Show how dataflow and memory shape an accelerator.

Parallelism

What changes when the work spans many devices? Explain a partitioning decision, a communication bottleneck, or a scaling experiment.

The practical part

Book once. Prepare asynchronously.

Choose a format and time, then add a title and a short description of what you’ll teach. You’ll receive a calendar invitation, a Zoom link, and a speaker-kit email automatically.

We handle hosting and audience preparation. Send your materials and any setup instructions by replying to the email; no preparation call is required. Use your own slides or the optional Vizuara template.

We’ll ask for your permission before recording or publishing your session.
